## Fix 1 — PM2.5 emission factors (RYTEM.json, EAR, base scenario)

### 1a. `PHL_HOU_COOK_OIL` ↔ `PHL_HOU_COOK_BIOM` values were swapped

The cooking techs consume fuel at IAR = 1, so these EARs are per PJ of fuel.
Converting to g/MJ and comparing with emission-inventory ranges (EMEP/EEA
Tier 1, household energy literature):

| Tech | Before | = g/MJ | Literature | After |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `HOU_COOK_OIL` | 7.36e-4 | 0.74 | kerosene/LPG 0.005–0.05; **wood 0.3–0.7** | **1.1e-5** |
| `HOU_COOK_BIOM` | 1.1e-5 | 0.011 | **wood 0.3–0.7**; kerosene/LPG-like value | **7.36e-4** |
| `HOU_COOK_COAL` | 1.18e-4 | 0.118 | household coal 0.1–0.25 ✓ | unchanged |

Coal being in-range while oil and biomass held each other's plausible values
indicates the two were entered into each other's rows. Effect before the fix:
switching households from fuelwood to LPG/kerosene *raised* model PM2.5 ~70× —
the inverse of the clean-cooking health rationale. The fix swaps the values
(flat across all years; this also removes a stray 0 in BIOM's 2053 cell).

### 1b. Transport PM2.5 factors ×1000 too large (unit slip)

The eight `PHL_TRA_*_LIQ` PM2.5 EARs are per 10⁹ km of activity, but the
entered numbers match typical **grams per vehicle-km** figures. 0.775 g/km
converts to 7.75e-4 Mt per 10⁹ km — a factor-1000 difference. As entered,
five road modes alone implied ~59 Mt PM2.5/yr in 2020; the entire Philippine
national inventory (all sources) is well under 1 Mt/yr. All eight values were
multiplied by 1e-3:

| Tech(s) | Before | After | Sanity (per vkm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TRUL/TRUH/VAN/BUS_LIQ` | 0.775 | 7.75e-4 | 0.775 g/vkm — plausible older HDV |
| `CAR_LIQ` | 0.0125 | 1.25e-5 | 0.0125 g/vkm — plausible LDV |
| `23WHEEL_LIQ` | 0.125 | 1.25e-4 | 0.125 g/vkm — plausible 2-stroke trike |
| `RAILF/RAILP_LIQ` | 0.002475 | 2.475e-6 | 2.5 g/train-km scaled |

The occupancy/basis refinement was applied the same day as **Fix 3** (below).
The FSH factors (set 2026-07-17) and power-sector factors were checked and are
in sane ranges.

## Fix 2 — deleted orphan technology `PHL_PWR_GAS`

Evidence it was vestigial: consumed cooling water (`PHL_PWR_WAT`, IAR 0.03)
with **no output commodity**, no costs, no emissions, no residual capacity, not
a member of any user-defined constraint, and zero activity in every year of
the solved `Base_v9` run. It was also the only tech named `PWR_*` rather than
`POW_*` — likely a leftover from an early model version, superseded by
`POW_CHP_NG_OLD` (existing gas fleet) and `POW_PP_NGCC` (new build). Removed
from `genData.json` with all parameter rows regenerated by the app's own
UpdateCase logic (tech count 139 → 138). The cooling-water balance is
unaffected: all 11 real thermal plants still consume `PHL_PWR_WAT`.

## Fix 3 — transport activity-basis reconciliation (PM2.5 occupancy + labels)

Back-calculating each mode's fuel IAR against typical per-vehicle fuel
intensities revealed that the transport module **mixes activity bases by
mode**, and the demand magnitudes plus the ~420 PJ total (matching the PH
transport energy balance) confirm it:

| Basis (as built) | Modes | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| passenger-km / tonne-km | car (occ. ~1.7), 2/3-wheeler (~1.6), light truck (~1.85 t), shipping | IAR = vehicle MJ/km ÷ occupancy; demand magnitudes match national pkm/tkm |
| VEHICLE-km | bus, van/jeepney, heavy truck | IAR ≈ vehicle MJ/km (implied occupancy ~1.2–1.3, physically absurd for buses); 3.82e9 "pkm" of bus travel only makes sense as vkm |
| TRAIN-km | rail passenger & freight | IAR = 176 MJ/km ≈ diesel train; 1.74M km/yr ≈ PNR scale |

Consequences applied:
1. **EAR division for the true-pkm/tkm modes only** (keeps emissions on the
   same basis as energy, using the same divisors embedded in the IARs):
   `CAR_LIQ` 1.25e-5 → **7.35e-6** (÷1.7); `23WHEEL_LIQ` 1.25e-4 → **7.81e-5**
   (÷1.6); `TRUL_LIQ` 7.75e-4 → **4.19e-4** (÷1.85).
   Bus/van/heavy-truck/rail were **not** divided — on a vehicle-km basis the
   Fix-1b values are already correct.
2. **Explicit `[basis: ...]` tags** appended to all nine transport demand
   commodity descriptions, so nobody pastes a pkm forecast into a vkm demand
   (doing so for buses would silently inflate bus energy ~25×).

Pre-fix-3 copies of `RYTEM.json`/`genData.json`: `pre-fix3-backup/`; script:
`fix3_occupancy_labels.py` (both under `tmp/model-fixes-2026-07-17/`).
Aviation was left untouched — its IAR (12.4 MJ/km) fits neither pkm nor
aircraft-km cleanly and needs the model owner's intent.

## Fix 5 — corrected `RENEWABLES` constraint membership

The RE scenario's annual renewable-share constraint uses the documented share
form, with coefficient `+s` for non-qualifying generation and `-(1-s)` for
qualifying renewable generation. The coefficient series in `RYTCn.json` had
been assigned to the wrong technology rows. The following source coefficients
were corrected for 2026–2053:

- `PHL_POW_PP_HY_LA`, `PHL_POW_GEO_OLD`, and `PHL_POW_PP_BIOM_CCS` now use
  `-(1-s)` and count toward the renewable target.
- `PHL_POW_PP_COAL`, `PHL_POW_PP_COAL_CCS`, and
  `PHL_POW_CHP_COAL_OLD` now use `+s` and do not count toward the renewable
  target.
- The anomalous 2053 cells for `PHL_POW_PP_NGCC` and
  `PHL_POW_PP_NGCC_CCS` were changed from `-0.305` to `+0.695`, keeping both
  technologies on the non-qualifying side throughout the horizon.

Nuclear was initially left neutral in this constraint during the membership
correction. It was subsequently added to the non-qualifying side as part of
**Fix 6** below, so nuclear generation is included in total generation without
receiving renewable credit. Hydrogen remains non-qualifying. Solar, wind, and
old biomass CHP were already on the qualifying side; old gas and oil CHP were
already on the non-qualifying side.

Semantic comparison with the pre-fix backup confirms exactly 170 changed year
cells: 28 for each of the six reclassified technologies and one 2053 cell for
each NGCC technology. No technology IDs, constraint metadata, other scenarios,
or other parameters changed. Existing solved outputs under `res/PEP_v9/` were
not refreshed; re-run `PEP_v9` before interpreting its capacity mix, activity,
objective, or constraint duals.

## Fix 6 — nuclear accounting and exact PEP capacity milestones

The PEP treats nuclear deployment separately from the renewable-energy target.
The model now reflects that distinction in both the renewable-share equation
and the capacity assumptions.

### 6a. Nuclear included in the RE-share denominator, not the numerator

`PHL_POW_PP_NUSMR` and `PHL_POW_PP_NU` now use the non-qualifying
`UDCMultiplierActivity` coefficient `+s` in the `RENEWABLES` constraint for
2026–2053. Nuclear generation therefore increases total electricity generation
used to calculate the renewable share, but it does not count as renewable
generation. The coefficients run from `+0.262` in 2026 to `+0.695` in 2053,
matching the other non-qualifying generators.

### 6b. Exact aggregate nuclear-capacity targets at PEP deadlines

A separate user-defined constraint, `NUCLEAR_CAPACITY_TARGET`, applies to the
sum of total capacity from the two nuclear technologies:

`PHL_POW_PP_NUSMR + PHL_POW_PP_NU = target`

The equality is active only in the three PEP deadline years:

| Deadline year | Exact aggregate nuclear capacity |
|---|---:|
| 2032 | 1.2 GW |
| 2035 | 2.4 GW |
| 2050 | 4.8 GW |

The OSeMOSYS user-defined constraint is encoded with `UDCTag = 1`, total-capacity
multipliers of `-1` for both nuclear technologies, and constants of `-1.2`,
`-2.4`, or `-4.8`. This is algebraically equivalent to the positive equality
shown above. The multipliers and constant are nonzero only in 2032, 2035, and
2050; in every other year they resolve to zero, making the equality `0 = 0`.
The model can therefore commission SMR capacity whenever it chooses before a
deadline, provided aggregate nuclear capacity lands exactly on that deadline's
target. Capacity after the final 2050 deadline is not capped by this PEP
constraint.

The target is aggregate rather than technology-specific: any residual
conventional-nuclear capacity counts toward the milestone and SMR capacity
supplies the remainder. There is no separate upper or lower capacity trajectory
on either nuclear technology. Each equality fixes aggregate capacity only at
its deadline, rather than holding that value throughout the following period.

### 6c. No new conventional-nuclear construction

`TotalAnnualMaxCapacityInvestment` for `PHL_POW_PP_NU` is set to zero in every
year of the RE scenario. This prohibits all additional conventional-nuclear
construction while leaving any `ResidualCapacity` untouched. The current
conventional-nuclear residual-capacity series is zero, but the formulation
deliberately preserves any existing capacity if that input is later updated.

### 6d. Implementation history and generated inputs

The first implementation fixed the milestones directly on SMR using equal
technology-level minimum and maximum capacity bounds and capped total
conventional-nuclear capacity at zero. That was superseded because it allocated
all nuclear capacity to one technology and represented the milestones as a
fixed SMR trajectory. A subsequent aggregate minimum-capacity constraint was
also superseded, as was an aggregate equality repeated in every year between
milestones. The final implementation applies the aggregate equality only in
the three deadline years described in 6b, plus the new-investment restriction
in 6c.

The source changes are in `genData.json`, `RYCn.json`, `RYTCn.json`, and
`RYT.json`. `res/PEP_v9/data.txt` and `data_processed.txt` were regenerated to
reflect them. No optimization was run: `results.txt`, `lp.lp`, and the existing
CSV result files remain from the preceding solve and must not be interpreted as
results of this nuclear formulation.

## Fix 7 — Fisheries v2 energy-balance and Industry-boundary recalibration

The first Fisheries calibration used a bottom-up fleet estimate that implied
about **19.2 PJ of fishing-vessel fuel alone**. This exceeded the Philippine
DOE's 2023 control total of **240.0 ktoe = 10.04832 PJ** for the entire
operational Fishery subsector. Version 2 replaces that estimate with a
top-down DOE-normalized balance and applies it to the model's 2020 base year as
a representative recent operating year.

The detailed DOE balance reports 103.65 ktoe of Fishery petroleum. The model's
generic liquid commodity also includes an estimated 2.145 ktoe biodiesel share,
allocated in proportion to reported diesel use. Electricity is the residual to
the 240.0 ktoe control total. The resulting operational balance is:

| Carrier | 2020-normalized final energy |
|---|---:|
| Generic liquid fuel | **4.429425 PJ** |
| Electricity | **5.618895 PJ** |
| **Operational Fishery total** | **10.048320 PJ** |

The accounting boundary is now explicit:

- `PHL_FSH_MOT` and `PHL_FSH_AQC` are inside the DOE Fishery control total.
- `PHL_FSH_PRO` represents ice, refrigeration, canning, drying and other
  post-harvest processing and is classified inside Industry.
- Feed milling remains in Industry and is excluded from FSH aquaculture.
- An energy-equivalent processing amount is deducted from `PHL_INDU_OTH`, so
  explicit fish processing is not counted a second time in aggregate Industry.

### 7a. Recalibrated base-year activities

| Service/technology | Final input | IAR | Useful activity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `PHL_FSH_MOT_LIQ` | 4.077425 PJ liquid | 2.80 | 1.456223214 PJ |
| `PHL_FSH_MOT_ELE` | 0 | 1.30 | 0 |
| `PHL_FSH_AQC_LIQ` | 0.352000 PJ liquid | 3.30 | 0.106666667 PJ |
| `PHL_FSH_AQC_ELE` | 5.618895 PJ electricity | 1.15 | 4.885995652 PJ |
| `PHL_FSH_PRO_LIQ` | 1.250 PJ liquid | 1.60 | 0.781250000 PJ |
| `PHL_FSH_PRO_ELE` | 0.860 PJ electricity | 1.10 | 0.781818182 PJ |

The motive-liquid IAR changed from 3.7 to **2.8**, consistent with a DOE fuel
mix that is more than 96% diesel by energy rather than the gasoline-heavy mix
assumed in v1. Its PM2.5 EAR was reduced from `3.0e-4` to **`9.1e-5` Mt/PJ
useful** using the same diesel basis. Residual capacities were recalculated from
the new activities, `CAU = 31.536`, and the existing availability factors.

Matching 2020 `TAL` and `TAU` values now pin the observed technology activities.
This prevents the optimizer from ignoring residual capacity and replacing the
calibrated carrier split with whichever technology is cheapest. The point
constraints are released after 2020, so future fuel switching remains
endogenous.

### 7b. Industry double-counting correction

The explicit processing block uses **2.11 PJ** of final energy in 2020. The
saved aggregate Industry calibration implies 1.537442 PJ final energy per PJ
of `PHL_INDU_OTH` service. The corresponding Industry service deduction is
therefore 1.372409 PJ in 2020, increasing with processing demand to 3.640090 PJ
in 2053. `PHL_INDU_OTH` SAD changes from **207.489160 to 206.116751 PJ** in
2020 and from **434.381986 to 430.741896 PJ** in 2053. The auditable annual
series is stored in `FSH_industry_carveout.csv`.

### 7c. Verification and solve status

The Fisheries verifier passes all schema, scenario-inheritance, connectivity,
base-activity, residual-capacity, carrier-balance and Industry-carve-out checks
(138 technologies, 61 commodities, four scenarios and 34 years). A temporary
case copy also passed the app's data-generation/preprocessing pipeline and the
GLPK matrix check.

A full temporary CBC optimization was attempted but stopped after roughly
15 minutes because it remained CPU-bound and had not returned a solution. The
existing `res/Base_v9/` solution was **not modified** and predates this
recalibration; it must not be interpreted as a Fisheries-v2 result. A fresh
scenario solve is still required before reporting system costs, capacity mix,
emissions or future Fisheries technology choices.
